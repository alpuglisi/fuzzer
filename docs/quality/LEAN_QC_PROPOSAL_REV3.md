### Three explicit requirements to incorporate into the existing policy

**1. Systemic-diagnosis trigger — L01/L02/L11.** When the same failure mechanism recurs across routes, consumers or applications, or a bounded experiment exhausts its declared budget without its predicted stage improvement, the delivery owner must disposition the approach before commissioning another equivalent local patch. Check existing evidence first; if it is insufficient, use the smallest discriminating experiment. Record one of: isolated defect with evidence, missing shared contract, broader capability gap, or unresolved hypothesis. Select the next action accordingly, with a fresh prediction and budget. A material safety exposure receives immediate containment regardless of this trigger. Do not require a new architecture document, committee or rewrite; an evidenced decision in the existing change record is sufficient. An ordinary first isolated defect does not trigger this requirement.

**2. Evidence for design-critical assumptions — L08/L10.** Before relying on an assumption to implement a capability change, link the decisive source/call path, authoritative specification or controlled observation that supports it, and state material uncertainty. Examples include which function actually calls a policy, whether the browser submits credentials, and which headers the relevant runtime/library sends. If evidence is missing, test the premise within the experiment budget and label it unverified until resolved. Block claims of validated design or completion that depend on an unresolved premise; do not block a bounded exploratory spike whose purpose is to resolve it. The reviewer challenges the premise using a counterexample or primary evidence. A schema can verify that a link exists; it cannot establish that the link proves the claim.

**3. Capability-transfer acceptance — L06/L07/L09.** A claim to fix a general technology capability must pass a predeclared behavior contract on the diagnostic regression and independently authored variations covering the relevant mechanism. Vary misleading incidental details, such as route vocabulary, routing mode, login presentation or request-body shape, while retaining the supported semantics. Use equivalent auth, configuration, target state and budget. Select coverage by the claim and risk, not a compulsory number of labs. Production discovery must not depend on lab identity, answer keys or undisclosed target hints; supported protocol/framework adapters remain legitimate when their contracts are explicit and tested independently. Keep previously working cases and safety controls in qualification. Cases exposed through tuning become regression data; use fresh evaluator-controlled cases for a subsequent unbiased transfer estimate. Passing one test cannot justify “works in any lab.”

These requirements belong in the existing short operating policy and affected technical contracts. They do not create three new control families, three new forms or three additional approval stages. A single short decision can satisfy more than one requirement when the evidence genuinely overlaps. Keep the original PA history and IDs; do not append an action for every variation of the same mechanism.

### Accountability, enforcement and effectiveness

The delivery owner applies the systemic trigger; the component owner substantiates the design premise; the existing risk-appropriate reviewer/evaluator checks acceptance evidence. One person may hold multiple roles where risk permits. Reviewer independence concerns the basis for the judgment; extra agreeing agents are not a substitute for independent evidence.

Use existing run manifests and the change record to expose goal, hypothesis, predicted stage movement, result, cumulative effort and disposition. Automate missing-field/run-identity checks and transfer/regression execution where useful. Keep the technical interpretation with a qualified reviewer. Do not introduce keyword guards claiming to recognize “architectural thinking.” If an alarm fires, narrow or change the authorized approach and report the obstacle; seek a user decision only where the actual scope, budget authority or material risk requires one.

Before claiming recurrence prevention, challenge the design with four counterexamples: (a) a renamed login path defeats a proposed lexical fix; (b) a false runtime/header assumption lacks supporting observation; (c) commits and green unit tests accumulate while the accepted capability stays unchanged; (d) user contact or an item rename occurs during an exhausted experiment. The controls must expose the unsupported premise or failed outcome and produce the appropriate disposition while accepting a legitimate isolated correction. A paper replay qualifies control logic only. Also observe actual application over comparable deliveries, including triggered opportunities, and review accepted capability, rework, lead time and safety outcomes. If no relevant trigger occurs, retain provisional effectiveness status rather than treating silence as proof.

**Recommendation:** retain the lean proposal with these explicit requirements and qualify it through a bounded pilot. The primary remaining work is implementing and proving the controls, not writing more policy. ### What remains valid, and what should not be added

Retain the 12 control families, proportionate CAPA, one authoritative change record, affected verification with a final integration backstop, qualified cache reuse, preservation of history, and the removal of misleading activity quotas. The autopsy strengthens those recommendations; it does not justify a return to mandatory unanimous multi-agent review, universal full-suite reruns, a new form for every defect, or deletion of safety controls. No change to the historical Appendix A disposition counts is warranted by this reassessment; the strengthened operating requirements apply across the mapped families.

The goal is general capability across declared supported technologies. A development lab may expose a technology gap, but tuning to its route names or answer key does not establish transfer. Equally, promising success on every possible application would be untestable. State the supported conditions and test variations that could falsify that claim.

### Revised first recovery cycle

1. **Establish trustworthy feedback.** Select one merged SHA, target/reset, generic command, dependencies, authorization/refusal policy and known auth state. Preserve the baseline artifacts. Produce per-case funnel evidence, including previously working cases. If the baseline cannot be reconstructed, establish a new one and label historical comparisons uncertain.
2. **Run one discriminating slice.** Use an evaluator-authored SPA login and one benign JSON action. Compare automated authentication with safely adopted authenticated state to isolate auth from discovery. Compare current discovery with the smallest observation change only if that distinction is needed. Record predicted stage movement and the effort ceiling before running. This is an example experiment, not a mandated architecture or a new universal test burden.
3. **Make an explicit technical decision.** Preserve required containment. If the predicted stage does not move, change or narrow the hypothesis. Do not initiate another broad redesign merely because a review can generate more concerns. Use a task-specific experiment budget; the autopsy's suggested two experiments is an alarm example, not another rigid count quota.
4. **Check transfer and losses.** Evaluate equivalent auth/configuration on independently authored variations and fresh cases not used for tuning. Adjudicate unmatched findings; retain inconclusive labels until evidence resolves them. Passing the slice qualifies that hypothesis, not the whole fuzzer.
5. **Verify the corrective controls in use.** Challenge budget/scope resets and actual hook invocation, then observe whether comparable bounded deliveries finish with less rework and no unacceptable quality loss. Retain unresolved status until that evidence exists.

A thin qualification cycle is the first work product. Reorganizing all 162 historical entries is not a prerequisite to it. Baseline collection should reuse machine-produced artifacts and the existing change record rather than launch another reporting project.

### Six Sigma and compliance assessment

In DMAIC terms, the first proposal emphasized Improve and Control before the measurement system was adequately qualified. Revision 2 restores the sequence: define acceptance, qualify measurement, discriminate causes, test the intervention, and verify effectiveness. For this software process, measurement qualification means reliable case identities, comparable run conditions, reproducible stage classification and independent adjudication; it does not require a ceremonial manufacturing gauge study.

The highest-priority nonconformity is the inability to demonstrate that completed work meets the accepted objective. Scope/authorization ambiguity and a corrective guard that clears on mere contact are additional control defects. Duplicate records and repeated setup are waste amplifiers. Releasing faster while retaining the first defects would optimize the wrong process.

Closure requires the causal mechanism addressed, the control implemented and qualified, and operating evidence meeting a predeclared effectiveness criterion over relevant opportunities. A patch, a named test or another written PA cannot alone close the systemic incident. Define the observation window by risk and opportunity frequency before assessing results; sparse observations support provisional status, not certification. No applicable external regulatory obligation or violation has been established by this review.

**Approval recommendation:** adopt this revised operating design as a bounded pilot under the project's existing authority. Do not declare the incident resolved or remove existing required gates solely because this proposal is accepted. The accountable owner receives a concise acceptance result, residual risks, comparative delivery cost and control-effectiveness evidence before broader adoption.

## Management judgment

**The quality system has become an accumulating incident history used as a mandatory work instruction. That is poor process design.** Useful engineering controls are buried among duplicates, completed corrections, local workarounds, review-round amendments and instructions about agent conduct. Every recurrence tends to add another rule without retiring the old active wording. The resulting control system increases reading, coordination, documentation and verification work without demonstrating a corresponding improvement in shipped quality.

**Some requirements are outright counterproductive as written.** The two-unique-CWE quota can incentivize inaccurate classification. Counting edited assertions measures activity rather than correctness. A blanket rollback promise after atomic rename is impossible. A separate pytest invocation merely to prove a file ran duplicates evidence already available from the same committed-tree run. These should be replaced or rewritten, not defended because they originated in a bug report.

**The file itself has not been shown to slow application execution.** Its only reference found under `fuzzlab/` is a resolver docstring; the inspected runtime does not load the action register as an executable rule engine. Controls implemented because of the register can have runtime costs, but attributing those costs requires profiling. The strong available evidence concerns **engineering delivery time**: repeated installs, broad verification, sequential gates, expanding review loops and documentation rework.

The recommended response is to preserve the effective safeguards, shrink the active instruction set, qualify controls by risk and failure mechanism, and collect evidence once. Do not delete security checks or scientific-validity tests to make the numbers look faster.

### Current process mechanisms that can amplify those costs

1. **An unconditional three-party design gate.** `docs/components/README.md` requires two reviewer agents and unanimous agreement before substantive implementation. Every concern is revised and re-reviewed. The gate is not scaled by risk. A targeted correction can wait behind the same process as a new credential trust boundary.
2. **A new preventive action for every code defect.** `CLAUDE.md` and `docs/bugs/README.md` require ERROR_LOG, a structured RCA record, a new PA, recurrence review and a codebase sweep for every defect. Component change logs and the changelog add more records. Requirements/architecture updates are conditional, but the core record burden is universal.
3. **Active rules cannot be retired normally.** `CLAUDE.md` says the PA register grows and rules are not deleted. Historical evidence and current instructions are consequently mixed. PA-0030 and PA-0039 explicitly say they are superseded, while PA-0055 explicitly says it adds no new rule.
4. **Verification selects mixed test files as if all were cheap guards.** `verify_change.py` selects whole files containing `ast.parse`, Python-file globbing or `os.walk`. A fast AST rule can pull in that file's model-training, browser or other behavioral tests. Even no package/test changes select 48 files, including `test_ml_holdout_seal.py`, `test_browser_scope.py`, `test_session_browser_login.py` and `test_packaging.py`.
5. **Verification also contains a host-wide waiting point.** The harness uses a host lock and waits for unrelated pytest processes. This protects shared resources where necessary, but becomes a queue for isolated tests too. Any future relaxation must first prove resource isolation; running competing shared-lab suites blindly would be unsafe.
6. **The Stop hook uses the language of incidents as a proxy for actual incidents.** It can block a routine timeout description and is satisfied when ERROR_LOG appears among touched files. That does not establish the presence or adequacy of a genuine incident record.
7. **The new orchestration limits contain their own ambiguity.** The guard counts individual reviewer requests as rounds; two reviewers can consume two of the three allowed requests in one conceptual round. Any later user message can reset budget/round/authorization gates. A status question is not an approval decision.

These are causal mechanisms or demonstrated false-positive controls. A before/after value-stream measurement is still needed to allocate the share of total delay to each. A smaller Markdown file alone will not remove requirements enforced in CLAUDE.md, component policy, hooks and the verification harness.

### Documentation and evidence-control defects

- The register begins by promising no background, but several entries contain hundreds of words of historical defense, examples and review amendments. PA-0037 alone has 413 whitespace-delimited words; PA-0130 has 396. These belong in incident records or technical design contracts.
- IDs are not in a simple order and have intentional/allocated gaps and ranges. That is not itself a defect; forcing renumbering would break traceability. Keep IDs immutable and generate a topic/status index.
- “Enforced by none” conflates manual review, hooks, autouse fixtures, on-host tests, supersession and genuinely missing enforcement. Replace it with explicit mechanism and evidence status.
- A named function's existence does not prove a whole PA is enforced. For example, PA-0007's cited cookie-predicate test is narrower than the full differential-authentication rule. The register checker should not be advertised as an effectiveness checker.
- Requirements mix different abstraction levels: invariant, implementation, historical correction, project deliverable, agent behavior and management escalation. That makes applicability unclear.
- Several rules use unbounded terms such as every foreseeable failure, every grammar ambiguity, every field, and every function. Define supported inputs, scope and failure outcomes rather than making untestable universal promises.

## QA and Lean Six Sigma redesign

The quality objective is **less total defect cost and faster delivery of verified behavior**. Neither maximum rule count nor maximum test count is the objective. From a QA management perspective, the present system overuses formal CAPA: a correction to one defect, a systemic corrective action, an engineering standard and a formal investigation are treated almost interchangeably.

Use mistake-proofed shared primitives and representative behavior checks wherever possible; use inspection where prevention cannot eliminate the error. This follows ASQ's hierarchy of eliminating, replacing or simplifying error-prone steps before adding detection. [ASQ mistake proofing](https://asq.org/quality-resources/mistake-proofing). Risk-based CAPA specifically addresses avoiding disproportionate investigation burden. [ASQ risk-based corrective/preventive action](https://asq.org/quality-resources/webcasts/a-risk-based-corrective-preventive-action-strategy-webcast?id=b06c5a68228e41fea38f26a07560b04f).

### 1. Replace the global incident list with 12 applicable control families

Twelve is a proposed organizational structure, not a statistically optimized number. Preserve the old PA/BUG IDs in a searchable archive and map each to its current control. The active policy should be about 150–250 lines plus a generated applicability table; that is a usability target, not a mandatory quota. Contributors consult only affected technical contracts in addition to the short policy.

### 2. Use a risk-based review and verification route

| Change class | Examples | Review | Required verification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Administrative / nonbehavioral | Correct a link or wording; generated record refresh | Owner self-check; independent review only if an active obligation changes | Markdown/record schema/link checks. Changing a safety requirement is not a harmless docs edit. |
| Low-risk local correction | Display text, isolated pure transformation with stable interface | One owner or independent reviewer under delegated policy | Changed unit/contract tests plus cheap structural checks. |
| Medium-risk behavior or shared contract | New CLI action, emitter shape, shared normalization | One independent qualified reviewer; domain specialist where needed | Affected downstream contracts, representative integration, owned guards; merged-tree integration as appropriate. |
| High-risk safety/data integrity | Credential origins, destructive sends, imported schemas, holdout membership, durability | Independent domain/security review before implementation and before release when evidence changes | Relevant adversarial/concurrency/real-browser tests, explicit risk decision and full qualification of the final candidate tree. |

Do not use severity multiplication into a simplistic risk-priority number to waive catastrophic safety failures. Record consequence, exposure/change reach and detectability separately; high-severity exposure cannot be cancelled by a favorable average score. Preserve user-required approval gates until the project owner adopts a revised policy.

Replace automatic two-reviewer unanimity with the table above. One review cycle should return a single consolidated must-fix list. A second round verifies those fixes. A third round is a signal to clarify scope/design, not an invitation to keep generating unrelated improvements. Unresolved material concerns go to the accountable owner; cosmetic preferences do not reset the whole gate.

For uncertain capability or architectural changes, independent review must challenge the premise and the proposed observation, not just inspect the patch. Give the reviewer the acceptance contract and primary evidence, and ask for a falsifying case. Several agents sharing the coordinator's assumptions do not provide independent primary evidence. A specialist or different evaluator can be appropriate for material uncertainty; an extra model or reviewer is not a universal gate. Acceptance checks and code review answer different questions and should reuse evidence where appropriate.

### 3. Select fast feedback without hiding defects

Proposed verification layers:

1. **Fast structural layer:** deterministic lint, schema/traceability reference checks and safety-boundary discovery. Split these from mixed behavioral files. Parse each unchanged source file once per worker/content hash and run multiple rules over the shared AST. Cache immutable AST data; do not cache mutable test state or suppress behavior tests.
2. **Affected contract layer:** select production importers, shared-primitive consumers, data/template/schema consumers, and changed tests. Add explicit dependency metadata where imports cannot express the relation. The existing transitive option and data-file ownership are useful starting points, not a complete guarantee.
3. **Target-runtime layer:** affected browser, emitter, process and live-tool suites in qualified isolated environments. Reuse immutable dependency installations and build artifacts. Each test still gets private application state, ports and writable files.
4. **Merged-candidate backstop:** one full qualification of the final integration tree before release/push where the adopted policy requires it. Periodic full runs compare selector coverage and find unmapped dependencies. A miss becomes a selector/contract-map defect, not an argument to run every expensive test after every keystroke.

Store a result manifest keyed by **commit/tree, dependency lock hashes, interpreter/toolchain, relevant environment/flags, selected test IDs and test inputs**. Reuse evidence only for that exact qualified state. Running the same unchanged tree/environment twice because one contributor distrusts another summary is avoidable; checking the signed/identified output is different from trusting the summary. Any code or relevant environment change invalidates the affected evidence.

For capability evaluation, extend that generated manifest with target version/reset, authentication outcome, authorization/refusal policy, generic invocation and evaluator-key version. Record per-case discovery/reach, candidate generation, actual sending, observation and verdict, with explicit reasons for losses. Preserve case identities across runs so gains cannot hide regressions. A zero-attempt refusal cannot be explained by response handling. Compare equivalent conditions or label the result confounded; never convert unadjudicated findings to false positives. Keep evaluator-owned test cases separate from production discovery logic, and refresh unseen evaluation cases after tuning.

Separate fast static safety checks from runtime safety gates. Preserve independent defenses where they cover different failure modes. Run cold-cache qualification when dependencies/toolchains change, and warm-cache tests for ordinary iteration. Apply resource-class locks to genuinely shared resources; remove host-wide serialization only after tests demonstrate isolated ports, databases, process ownership and writable state.

Do not introduce automatic retries that turn a red test green silently. Store initial failures, classify environmental noise versus product/test defects, and require disposition. A temporary quarantine has an owner, deadline, risk statement and compensating gate; it never counts as passed.

### 4. One change record, multiple generated views

Use one source record in the PR/change system:

```yaml
id: CHANGE-<immutable-id>
goal: <observable user outcome>
scope: [<affected components and contracts>]
risk: low | medium | high
risk_reason: <consequence and exposure>
acceptance: [<specific observable conditions>]
hypothesis: <prediction, effort ceiling and exit decision; only for uncertain work>
implementation: <commit/tree>
verification: <result-manifest reference>
review: <reviewer, disposition, unresolved risk>
incident_links: [<existing bug/control IDs if relevant>]
control_status: <designed | implemented | qualified | operating-effective; if CAPA>
rollback_or_recovery: <required for meaningful state changes>
```

Generate changelog summaries, component history links and traceability indices from this source. Edit a living requirement only when the behavior/contract changes; do not duplicate the whole incident narrative in every component. Preserve immutable records and append dated corrections when a causal claim is revised. The latest commit's correction of BUG-0143's causal claim is a good example of why records must distinguish evidence from hypotheses.

For an ordinary defect, record reproduction, correction, regression check and affected scope. Escalate to formal CAPA when risk, recurrence, spread or a failed control warrants it. Formal CAPA adds verified cause, systemic action, owner, due date and an effectiveness plan. An additional PA is not mandatory when an existing control can be corrected or its applicability widened.

Use immutable underlying defect identities with aliases when human labels collide; never renumber history merely to tidy it. Generate a current-status view from dated corrections and evidence. Distinguish a proposed rule, an owner-adopted policy, implemented behavior, qualification evidence and observed effectiveness. Formal CAPA closure requires the predeclared effectiveness criterion, not only a green regression test. These are fields/views in the existing record, not additional universal forms or approval queues.

No applicable external regulation or certification requirement requiring this repository's current three-party/every-defect paperwork was established in the inspected material. These are internal project policies, including some attributed to prior direct user instructions. This proposal changes their design only if adopted; it does not silently waive them.

### 5. Measure effectiveness and total delivery cost

| Metric | Definition / guard against misuse | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Change lead time, p50/p90 | Accepted work to qualified completion; separate active effort and waiting | Identify review and verification queues. Do not compare unlike risk classes unadjusted. |
| Process-cycle efficiency | Necessary transformation/verification time divided by elapsed lead time, with classification agreed first | Expose waiting, duplicate reporting and rework. Necessary safety evidence is not automatically waste. |
| First-pass yield | Changes passing the required gate on first submission / submitted changes | Indicates quality at source; do not improve it by hiding first failures or reducing required gates. |
| Escaped defect rate and severity | Post-gate product defects per released change or usage opportunity, segmented by risk | Quality constraint on speed improvements. Track recurrence separately. |
| Rework and review demand | Hours and must-fix rounds attributable to a change, separated from new scope | Reveal expanding review loops and inaccurate impact analysis. |
| Verification cost | Wall time, compute time, cold/warm setup, collection, execution, teardown and queue time | Direct bottleneck measurement; complements algorithmic operation counts. |
| Control effectiveness | Failure-mode attacks detected / attempted, legitimate cases accepted, misses, applicability and last qualification | More meaningful than the percentage of rules with a test-name link. |
| Selector misses | Relevant failures found by full qualification but omitted by affected-suite selection | Validate the faster feedback route. A miss requires investigation of selection assumptions. |
| Outcome progress | Task-specific accepted milestones; for a fuzzer, valid unique findings with false-positive controls and budget | Avoid rewarding duplicate/noisy findings or interpreting necessary risk removal as zero value. |
| Measurement validity | Acceptance runs with complete identity, comparable preconditions and adjudicated case outcomes / attempted acceptance runs | Invalid or inconclusive comparisons remain visible; do not exclude them to inflate yield. |
| Capability stage losses | Cases lost at auth, discovery, candidate generation, policy refusal, send, observation or confirmation; gained and lost identities | Locate the failed assumption without treating reach as detection or refusal as a negative result. |
| Goal-level stall and effort | Cumulative effort and unchanged-since time on the immutable goal, with hypothesis dispositions | User contact, item renaming and coordinator changes cannot reset the history. An informed decision can grant a specified budget extension without erasing past spend. |

Use a run chart first. Establish stable definitions and enough comparable observations before applying control limits; do not report a Six Sigma level or capability index from these sparse/nonstationary delivery data. A five-change pilot is useful for finding process friction, not proof of equal escape rates.

**Pilot targets, not achieved results:** first establish valid acceptance measurements and observed qualified-control operation. Then aim to halve median routine-change lead time, bring the fast structural feedback lane below two minutes and routine affected suites below ten minutes where feasible, and reduce duplicate manual record entry to zero. Track p90, recurrence, escapes and safety outcomes alongside speed. Retain mandatory safety/evaluation checks; predefined unacceptable quality loss stops the pilot even if it is faster. Revisit targets after the baseline. Do not promise an application-runtime speedup without a profile.

| Stage | Work | Evidence to leave the stage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recover a measurable slice first | Qualify one baseline, isolate the highest-value uncertain mechanism, run a bounded experiment and test the corrective guard's decision semantics | Replayable per-case result and explicit hypothesis disposition; actual guard invocation checked where relied upon. | Baseline | Time the next comparable changes by review, setup, execution, queue, records and rework; capture cold/warm suite profiles | Comparable timeline and failure inventory, with no fabricated speed estimate. |
| Consolidate policy | Approve the 12-family operating policy; mark old PA entries active/superseded/archived with mappings and keep original IDs | 162/162 mappings; no orphaned safety invariant or contradictory active authority. |
| Consolidate records | Introduce one authoritative change record and generate the other views | Historic records preserved; required links and reviewer decisions reproducible. |
| Refactor verification | Split mixed guard files, share AST indexing, retain real behavioral suites, add dependency metadata and result manifests | Compare old/new selections; deliberate known offenders still fail; legitimate edits stop false-blocking. |
| Shadow pilot | Run fast selection for feedback while the existing final qualification remains authoritative | All fast-lane results attributable; no unexplained selector misses; observed review/record overhead measured. |
| Adopt | Owner accepts the risk-tier gates and controlled exceptions after pilot review | Explicit decision; tested rollback to prior gating; residuals and owners documented; observed control effectiveness and acceptance evidence, not just completed policy edits. |

Suggested initial ownership: delivery owner for L01/L11, verification owner for L09/L10/L12, and the affected component's technical owner for L02–L08. In a small project one person may hold several roles; role separation should match risk rather than manufacturing extra approval queues.

## Draft short operating instruction

This is a proposed replacement operating model, not an enacted change:

1. Define the observable user outcome, supported conditions, acceptance and authorized scope. Qualify the baseline measurement before comparing capability. Classify risk before choosing review and evidence.
2. Consult the affected current controls and technical contracts. Historical PAs are reference material, not 162 separate mandatory instructions for every change.
3. Validate impact by shared contract, state transition and consumer. Sequence conflicting semantic edits. Bound the investigation to the mechanism; report serious adjacent risks without silently expanding delivery scope.
4. For uncertain work, specify a falsifiable prediction, effort ceiling and exit decision. Build the smallest change/experiment that tests it and maintains safety/data integrity. Reuse qualified primitives and immutable setup.
5. Test changed behavior, relevant boundaries and affected consumers on an identified tree. Fast feedback and final qualification serve different purposes.
6. Have independent review proportional to risk, including a challenge to material causal/acceptance assumptions. Consolidate must-fix issues, park unrelated improvements, and change the hypothesis or escalate an unresolved obstacle within the agreed budget.
7. Record goal, risk, change, evidence and decision once. Generate other history views. Update living requirements when their contract changes.
8. Investigate significant or recurring defects as CAPA. Correct the existing control when possible; add one only for an uncovered mechanism. Record implementation, qualification and operating effectiveness separately; close against the predeclared effectiveness criterion.
9. Report outcome/stage changes, unchanged-since time, cumulative spend, unresolved risk and next experiment at the agreed cadence. Keep goal history across contact, renames and handoffs. A user message is not approval to expand scope or erase a budget; apply an informed decision only to its intended hold.
10. Preserve owned-process cleanup, scoped sending, private secrets, fail-closed safety state and honest evaluation in every route. Release only with the required evidence and explicit disposition of residual risk.

This is consistent with standardized work as a clear current sequence supported by cycle-time/capacity evidence, rather than an endlessly growing collection of reminders. [Lean Enterprise Institute, standardized work](https://www.lean.org/lexicon-terms/standardized-work/).
